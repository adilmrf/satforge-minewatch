MINEWATCH is a CubeSat proof-of-concept that detects floating naval mines, predicts where they will drift, and alerts ships and coast guards before they get there.

See the mission Why it matters

The problem

The region's busiest shipping lanes are mined, and nobody can see the whole picture.

~6,000 naval mines estimated in Iran's inventory
28 Feb 2026: Hormuz shipping largely blocked from this date
~6 mo estimated time to clear the strait (Apr 2026)
171 Houthi sea mines found & destroyed in the Red Sea at one point (2021)

Strait of Hormuz

In March 2026, Iran began laying mines in the strait. How many were laid has never been confirmed, but the threat alone was enough to stop tanker traffic and send insurance costs soaring. Uncertainty is itself the weapon.

Red Sea & Bab el-Mandeb

Most Houthi mines are floating or drifting, some built from gas cylinders and contact fuzes. Currents carry them into the main shipping lane toward Suez. They have struck cargo ships and killed fishermen.

The gap: mine clearance relies on ships, divers and drones that search a few square kilometres at a time. There is no persistent, wide-area picture of where floating mines are, or where they are heading.

The mission

Detect. Predict. Alert.

From low Earth orbit, a satellite sees thousands of kilometres of sea on every pass. MINEWATCH uses that view to flag floating objects in high-risk lanes, forecast their drift, and push hazard warnings to the people who need them.

  1. 01

    Detect

    An on-board camera scans the sea surface and flags floating objects that stand out from the water.

  2. 02

    Locate

    Satellite attitude and orbit position turn each detection into a position on the map.

  3. 03

    Predict drift

    Successive sightings plus surface conditions give a heading and speed, projecting where the mine will be.

  4. 04

    Alert

    Hazards are downlinked and published to a live dashboard for ships, ports and coast guards.

Coast guard & navy

Cue mine-countermeasure assets to the right search areas.

Shipping & ports

Re-route around predicted hazard zones.

Fishing communities

Early warning in waters with no naval presence.

The proof-of-concept

A 1U CubeSat, a water tray, and a live dashboard.

Our prototype demonstrates the full pipeline on the bench: a mock mine floats on a water tray, the CubeSat spots it, and the alert appears on this dashboard within seconds.

Honest scope: a real 1U camera resolves roughly 7 m per pixel from orbit, so it cannot see a 1 m mine. This PoC proves the detection, drift-prediction and alerting pipeline. An operational system would need larger optics or a constellation, with drones confirming each candidate.

Roadmap

From proof-of-concept to a layered mine-warning network.

Phase 1 · Now

CubeSat PoC

Floating-mine detection, drift prediction and alerting, demonstrated end to end.

Phase 2

Buoys & drones

In-water sensors confirm candidates and find seabed and moored mines. The satellite relays their data from beyond the horizon.

Phase 3

Operational constellation

Larger-aperture satellites over Hormuz, the Gulf of Oman and the Red Sea, feeding a regional hazard map.

Mission control

Live dashboard

Real-time detections, predicted drift tracks, and satellite health: temperature, power and attitude.
